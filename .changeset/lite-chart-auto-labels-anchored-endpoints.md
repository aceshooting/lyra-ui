`lr-lite-chart`'s `max-labels="auto"` no longer crowds the first and last category labels. Those two labels are anchored toward the plot interior, so each one spends its whole width on the inner side of its tick. Automatic density assumed every label was centered on its tick, so both boundary pairs got about half the room of the interior pairs. On a wide chart of 144 `HH:mm` labels, `00:00` and `01:00` (and the last two labels) nearly touched in both directions. Automatic density now measures each label on the side it actually paints and spaces label centers evenly between the two boundary labels. The same check also fixes a quieter case: when there are few categories, even-index rounding could leave two interior labels a single category apart, such as `Mar` and `Apr` on a narrow twelve-month chart. Whenever the first and last labels fit side by side, every adjacent pair now keeps the documented 10px of breathing room under the deterministic 7px-per-character estimate. The first and last labels are still always shown, labels stay in source order, and RTL gets the same spacing. Because the previous picks often packed labels closer than 10px, wide or dense charts can now show several fewer labels than before, and different interior labels may be picked. An explicit numeric `max-labels` value is unchanged.
